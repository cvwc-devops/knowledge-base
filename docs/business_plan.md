# Business Plan Requirements for Enterprise Board / Local Enterprise Office Funding in Ireland

In Ireland, the old “Enterprise Board” support structure is now mainly handled through the **Local Enterprise Offices, or LEOs**. There are 31 Local Enterprise Offices across Ireland supporting small businesses. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

Funding requirements vary by county and grant type, but most LEO funding applications expect a clear business plan, financial projections, evidence of market demand, and proof that the business is eligible.

---

## 1. Basic Eligibility Checks

Before preparing the plan, confirm that the business fits the funding category.

### Typical LEO Grant Eligibility

The business should usually be:

- A **micro-enterprise**
- Employing **10 people or fewer**
- Based in Ireland
- Trading, or preparing to trade, in a realistic commercial market
- Capable of growth
- Not already fully funded from another public source for the same costs
- Able to show commercial viability

A **Priming Grant** is aimed at micro-enterprises within the first **18 months** of start-up. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

A **Business Expansion Grant** is aimed at businesses in the growth phase after the first **18 months** of trading. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

Enterprise Ireland is usually for more ambitious start-ups, especially High Potential Start-Ups with international growth potential. HPSUs are expected to have potential for an innovative product or service, international sales, 10 jobs, and €1 million in sales within 3 years. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

## 2. Required Business Plan Sections

A strong business plan for LEO / enterprise funding should include the following sections.

### 1. Executive Summary

Include:

- Business name
- Promoter / founder name
- Business location
- Short description of the product or service
- Current business stage
- Funding amount requested
- Total project cost
- What the funding will be used for
- Key commercial opportunity
- Expected jobs, sales, exports, or local economic impact

The Local Enterprise Office business plan guidance says the executive summary should detail the objectives of the business and the requirements of the business plan. :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}

---

### 2. Business Description

Include:

- Legal structure: sole trader, partnership, or limited company
- Company registration status
- Tax registration status
- Trading address
- Ownership structure
- Business background
- Mission and objectives
- Current trading position, if already trading

Attach where available:

- Certificate of incorporation
- Tax clearance certificate
- Promoter CVs

LEO guidance lists these items as common appendices, including certificate of incorporation, tax clearance certificate, and promoter CVs. :contentReference[oaicite:5]{index=5}

---

### 3. Product or Service

Explain:

- What the business sells
- The problem it solves
- Who buys it
- Why customers will pay for it
- What makes it different
- Current development stage
- Any intellectual property, licences, or certifications
- Future product or service roadmap

For technology, manufacturing, food, export, or regulated businesses, include extra detail on compliance, production, quality control, and scalability.

---

### 4. Market Analysis

Include:

- Target market
- Market size
- Market trends
- Customer segments
- Customer needs
- Evidence of demand
- Competitor analysis
- Pricing comparison
- SWOT analysis

LEO business plan guidance specifically expects market overview, trends, size and potential, competition, customers, market structure, and SWOT analysis. :contentReference[oaicite:6]{index=6}

---

### 5. Sales and Marketing Strategy

Include:

- Sales channels
- Customer acquisition plan
- Marketing activities
- Pricing model
- Distribution model
- Sales process
- Website / online strategy
- Social media or advertising plan
- Partnerships
- Sales targets

The Local Enterprise Office guidance includes market strategy headings such as objectives, product, price, place, and promotion. :contentReference[oaicite:7]{index=7}

---

### 6. Operations Plan

Include:

- Premises
- Equipment required
- Suppliers
- Production process
- Delivery process
- Stock management
- Technology systems
- Insurance
- Licences or permits
- Health and safety requirements
- Key operating risks

If funding is requested for equipment, include an equipment schedule showing:

- Item
- Supplier
- Cost excluding VAT
- VAT
- Total cost
- Purpose
- Whether quote is attached

LEO guidance lists an equipment schedule as a common appendix. :contentReference[oaicite:8]{index=8}

---

### 7. Management and Staffing

Include:

- Founder background
- Relevant experience
- Skills and qualifications
- Current staff
- Planned hires
- Role descriptions
- Salary costs
- Contractors or advisers
- Skills gaps

Promoter CVs are normally expected as supporting documents. :contentReference[oaicite:9]{index=9}

---

### 8. Financial Projections

Usually include at least **2 years** of projections, and in some cases 3 years.

Include:

- Profit and loss forecast
- Cash flow forecast
- Balance sheet forecast
- Sales assumptions
- Cost assumptions
- Gross margin
- Break-even point
- Funding requirement
- Promoter contribution
- Other funding sources
- VAT treatment
- Loan repayments, if relevant

LEO guidance lists Year 1 and Year 2 financial projections, including profit and loss, cash flow, and balance sheet. :contentReference[oaicite:10]{index=10}

---

### 9. Funding Requirement

Clearly state:

- Total project cost
- Amount requested from LEO / Enterprise Ireland
- Promoter contribution
- Bank funding, investor funding, or other sources
- What the funding will be spent on
- Why the funding is needed
- What happens if funding is not approved
- Expected business impact from the funding

Typical eligible cost categories may include:

- Capital equipment
- Salary costs
- Consultancy
- Innovation
- Marketing

For example, Dublin City LEO describes Priming Grant support as potentially covering salary costs, capital items, consultancy costs, innovation costs, and marketing costs. :contentReference[oaicite:11]{index=11}

---

### 10. Risk Analysis

Include:

- Commercial risks
- Cash flow risks
- Supplier risks
- Technical risks
- Regulatory risks
- Staffing risks
- Market-entry risks
- Competitor risks
- Mitigation plan for each risk

A funding body will want to see that the promoter understands the risks and has a practical plan to manage them.

---

### 11. Milestones and Outputs

Include measurable targets such as:

- Launch date
- First sales date
- Monthly sales targets
- Number of customers
- Jobs created
- Export sales
- Product development milestones
- Marketing milestones
- Break-even date

Use numbers where possible.

Example:

| Milestone | Target Date | Evidence |
|---|---:|---|
| Complete website launch | Month 1 | Live website |
| Buy production equipment | Month 2 | Supplier invoice |
| First 20 paying customers | Month 4 | Sales records |
| Hire part-time assistant | Month 6 | Employment contract |
| Reach monthly break-even | Month 10 | Management accounts |

---

## 3. Supporting Documents Checklist

Attach the following where available:

- Business plan
- Financial projections
- Cash flow forecast
- Profit and loss forecast
- Balance sheet forecast
- Founder / promoter CV
- Certificate of incorporation
- Tax clearance certificate
- Quotations for equipment or services
- Lease or premises agreement
- Insurance details
- Product photos or prototype details
- Market research
- Letters of intent from customers
- Confirmed orders
- Supplier quotes
- Bank statements, if requested
- Evidence of promoter contribution
- Company accounts, if already trading

---

## 4. What Funders Will Look For

The plan should prove:

- The business is eligible
- The promoter is capable
- There is real market demand
- The product or service is clearly defined
- The business can make money
- The financial forecasts are realistic
- The funding need is clear
- The grant will create measurable impact
- The business can survive after the funding is spent

---

## 5. Common Weaknesses to Avoid

Avoid:

- Vague market claims
- No customer evidence
- Over-optimistic sales forecasts
- Missing cash flow forecast
- No promoter contribution
- No supplier quotes
- Weak competitor analysis
- No clear use of funds
- Treating the grant as guaranteed income
- Ignoring VAT, tax, wages, or working capital
- Not explaining how the business will generate repeatable sales

---

## 6. Suggested Business Plan Structure

Use this structure:

1. Executive Summary
2. Business Description
3. Promoter Background
4. Product or Service
5. Market Analysis
6. Competitor Analysis
7. Sales and Marketing Strategy
8. Operations Plan
9. Staffing Plan
10. Financial Projections
11. Funding Requirement
12. Risk Analysis
13. Milestones
14. Appendices

## 7. Funding-Specific Notes

### Priming Grant

Use this if the business is generally within the first 18 months of start-up. It is aimed at micro-enterprises and may support start-up investment. :contentReference[oaicite:12]{index=12}

### Business Expansion Grant

Use this if the business is past the initial start-up phase and is growing after the first 18 months. :contentReference[oaicite:13]{index=13}

### Feasibility Grant

Use this if the business needs to research market demand, technical feasibility, or commercial viability before launch or major investment. Feasibility grants are designed to help promoters research market demand and examine whether a product or service is viable. :contentReference[oaicite:14]{index=14}

### Enterprise Ireland HPSU Supports

Use this route if the business has strong international growth potential, innovation, and the ability to scale quickly. Enterprise Ireland’s HPSU supports are generally for companies with the potential to create 10 jobs and €1 million in sales within 3 years. :contentReference[oaicite:15]{index=15}

---

## 8. Practical Recommendation

Before writing the full plan, contact the relevant Local Enterprise Office for your county. Funding rules, grant limits, eligible costs, and application forms can vary by location and grant type.

Prepare the business plan as if the funder is asking three questions:

1. Is this business viable?
2. Is this promoter capable?
3. Will public funding create clear economic value?

If the plan answers those three questions with evidence, numbers, and a clear funding use case, it will be much stronger.
